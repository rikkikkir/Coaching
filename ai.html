Who AI advice is for

Three kinds of people

Families and individuals

Personal projects where AI does the heavy work and you make the decisions.

  • A family archive you can search
  • Photo books and printed keepsakes
  • Notes, trip plans and research
  • A question-and-answer notebook on any topic you care about

Small businesses

For owners who keep hearing about AI and want a straight answer.

  • A walk through a normal week to find repeated work
  • One or two tools you choose together (for example ChatGPT, Claude, or the AI already inside your email), set up for your team
  • Written rules for what never goes into an AI tool
  • Hands-on teaching for the team, with a one-page guide for each tool

Nonprofits and groups

Volunteer groups, clubs, congregations and senior centers.

  • A talk or a hands-on workshop
  • A question-and-answer notebook built from your own documents (made with NotebookLM, a Google tool that answers questions from documents you choose)
  • Plain written guides for volunteers
Step by step

How AI advice works

  1. Listen

    You describe the work, the worries and the wish list. Rikki asks questions.

  2. A short written plan

    Plain words: what AI can do here, what AI cannot do, and what each tool costs.

  3. Set up and teach

    Rikki sets up the tools with you and teaches the people who will use them.

  4. A guide to keep

    You get written steps and house rules, so your team can keep going between visits.

Privacy first

Your information stays yours

Ten years in health care taught Rikki to treat private information with care.

  • Ask firstRikki asks before putting anything of yours into an AI tool.
  • Know where things goFor each tool, you learn what leaves your computer and what stays.
  • Keep the private things homeSensitive material can be handled by AI that runs on your own computer. That takes a recent computer, and the answers are simpler.
Honest limits

What Rikki does not do

  • No legal, medical or money adviceAI can help you prepare questions for a professional. AI does not replace one.
  • No privacy-law or security auditFor customer, health or donor records, check the rules with a professional first.
  • No promise that AI is always rightAI tools make mistakes. You learn how to catch them.
  • No sales pitchRikki sells no software and takes no commissions.
