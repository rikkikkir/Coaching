Lessons for people who did not grow up with a smartphone.

One teacher, one topic at a time, for one learner or two together. On your own device, at your own speed, with the steps written down to keep.

Who lessons are for

You, a parent, or the two of you together

Boomers and older adults

You have the phone, the tablet or the computer. You would like the device to feel less like a puzzle.

Grown children buying for a parent

Anyone who wants a patient teacher

Not a Boomer? No one checks birth years. Lessons are open to any adult who wants calm, clear help.

Topics

What a lesson can cover

Each lesson follows your own questions. These are common places to start.

Phone, tablet and computer basics

  • Calls, texts, group texts and contacts
  • Bigger text, louder sound, a calmer screen
  • Setting up a new device, or moving from an old one
  • Updates, storage and backups
  • Computer basics: the mouse, windows and the web browser

Email without the mess

  • Sending, replying and adding an attachment
  • Clearing out junk mail
  • Telling a real message from a fake one

Photos: find, share, print

  • Finding one photo among thousands
  • Sharing pictures with family and friends
  • Albums, prints and photo books

Video calls

  • Starting and answering a video call
  • Joining a call from a link, such as a doctor’s visit by video
  • Fixing “I can’t hear you” and “I can’t see you”

Staying safe online

  • Spotting scam calls, texts and emails
  • Safer password habits, and the way back into a locked account
  • What to do when something looks wrong

First steps with AI

  • What AI helpers such as ChatGPT and Claude are, in plain words
  • Asking for help with a note, a recipe or a trip plan
  • How to tell when an AI answer is wrong

WiFi, printers and files

  • Getting on WiFi, at home and away
  • Printing and scanning a page
  • Finding a file you saved last month

A project of your own

  • A printed photo book
  • A family history to hand down
  • A timeline of your life, built with AI as a helper

Where Rikki stops: Rikki teaches the device, not the medical, legal or money decision. Rikki does not sign in to bank, investment or medical accounts for you and never moves money. If you think a scam has already taken money, call your bank first, on the number printed on your card.

After every lesson

Your own instruction sheet

Forgetting a new skill within a week is common, at any age.

After a lesson, Rikki writes the steps down for you: large type, plain words, in the order you tap. Printed on paper or sent by email, as you prefer.

Over time the sheets add up to a small handbook for your own devices, written for one reader: you.

Step by step

How a lesson goes

  1. A short hello

    You tell Rikki what you want to learn and which device you have.

  2. One topic

    The lesson stays on one topic until that topic feels easy.

  3. You do the tapping

    Most of the time your hands stay on your device. When a set-up step needs Rikki to tap, Rikki says what and why first, and you watch.

  4. Steps to keep

    You get an instruction sheet for the things you practiced.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need to know anything before a first lesson?

No. Bring the device and your questions. If the device is still in the box, bring the box.

Which devices does Rikki teach?

Apple devices are home ground for Rikki: iPhone, iPad and Mac. Windows computers and Android phones are welcome too. Say which device you have when you write.

Will Rikki see my passwords?

No. You type your own passwords, and Rikki looks away. Rikki never keeps a copy of a password.

Does Rikki repair computers?

No. Rikki teaches and helps with set-up. For a cracked screen or a dead battery, a repair shop is the right place.

Can I buy lessons for my mother or father?

Yes, once your parent says yes to lessons. Tell Rikki a little about your parent: the device, what your parent wants to do, and what has been frustrating. Your parent sets the pace from there.

Will Rikki tell me how my parent’s lessons go?

Only what your parent agrees to share. What happens in a lesson stays between Rikki and the learner, unless the learner asks Rikki to pass something on.

What if memory is a worry?

Lessons can be shorter and can repeat as often as helps. A family member is welcome to sit in. Rikki is a teacher, not a care provider.

Is AI safe to use?

For everyday tasks, yes, with two habits. First: never type in passwords, bank or Social Security numbers, health details, or private facts about other people. Second: check important answers against a second source. Rikki teaches both habits in the first AI lesson.

What if I forget everything by next week?

Forgetting is common, at any age. The instruction sheet is there for that week, and repeating a lesson is welcome.

Ready to learn one new thing?

Tell Rikki what you want to learn. One sentence is enough.
