Taking the quiz for a parent? Answer as your parent would.

This quiz needs scripts turned on. Until then, the questions are listed below, each with the matching help.

For you, or for a parent

  1. Question 1Is something wrong right now?

    A caller or a message asking for money, gift cards or a code. An account you cannot open. A device that will not start.

    If yes: hang up on any caller who asks for money. Then call your bank on the number printed on your card.

  2. Question 2Does your phone, tablet or computer feel like a puzzle?

    Screens that jump. Sounds that will not stop. Words too small to read.

    If yes: Phone, tablet and computer basics

  3. Question 3Do the WiFi and the printer give you trouble?

    A connection that drops. Pages that will not print. A saved file that vanished.

    If yes: WiFi, printers and files

  4. Question 4Do you have more unread email than you can face?

    Hundreds of unread messages, or a small feeling of dread each time you open the mail.

    If yes: Email without the mess

  5. Question 5In the last month, has a call, text or email made you wonder, “Is this a scam?”

    A prize you never entered for. A bank you do not use. A relative in sudden trouble.

    If yes: Staying safe online

  6. Question 6If you forgot your email password today, could you get back in on your own?

    Here a No is the answer that points to a lesson. No shame in a No.

    If no: Staying safe online

  7. Question 7Do you wish you could start a video call without asking someone for help?

    Family. Old friends. A doctor’s visit by video.

    If yes: Video calls

  8. Question 8Are your best photos trapped inside your phone?

    Thousands of pictures, and no easy way to find, share or print the one you want.

    If yes: Photos: find, share, print

  9. Question 9Would you like to try an AI helper, with Rikki beside you?

    ChatGPT and Claude are two AI helpers: computer programs you can ask in plain words. Handy for a first draft of a note, a recipe or a trip plan, and sometimes wrong.

    If yes: First steps with AI

  10. Question 10Are there family stories you want written down before they are lost?

    A family history. A timeline of your life. A printed book of old photos.

    If yes: A project of your own

  11. Question 11Could you meet Rikki in or near Bozeman, Montana or Grand Rapids, Michigan?

    Yes means lessons can happen in person. No means lessons by video.

    Either way: tell Rikki your town

For a shop, club or nonprofit

  1. Question 1Does your business or group repeat the same tasks every week?

    Writing the newsletter. Updating the sign-up list. Answering the same question again and again.

    If yes: a walk through a normal week

  2. Question 2Do volunteers or staff ask for the same how-to again and again?

    How to post the schedule. How to add a member. How to use the shared calendar.

    If yes: plain written guides

  3. Question 3Is there a pile of documents nobody can search?

    Minutes. Handbooks. Policies. Years of newsletters.

    If yes: a question-and-answer notebook

  4. Question 4Is anyone already pasting work into ChatGPT or another AI tool?

    Letters. Lists of names. Notes from a meeting.

    If yes: house rules for AI

  5. Question 5Do you keep records about members, donors or customers?

    Names. Addresses. Gifts. Orders. Health or family details.

    If yes: privacy first

  6. Question 6Would the people in your group like a hands-on AI lesson, together?

    A talk for the whole club, or a workshop for a small team.

    If yes: a talk or a hands-on workshop

Not sure? Pick the closer answer. Any answer can be changed at the end.

Do this first

  • A caller or a message wants money, gift cards, a code or control of your computer? Stop and hang up. Then call your bank on the number printed on your card.
  • Locked out of an account, or a device that will not start? Write down the exact words on the screen. Those words help any helper find the fix.
  • Tell someone you trust what happened.

Then tell Rikki. The plan below puts safety first.

You are The Careful One.

Scammers work hard. A few steady habits make you a much harder target.

You are The Fresh Starter.

Your devices have been running the show. A few calm lessons put you back in charge.

You are The Troubleshooter.

The WiFi and the printer have had the upper hand. Time to learn their tricks.

You are The Untangler.

The inbox grew while you were busy living. One lesson clears the pile, and a few habits keep the pile small.

You are The Connector.

People come first for you. Technology should bring the faces you miss closer.

You are The Memory Keeper.

Your pictures matter. Time to get the best ones out of the phone and onto paper.

You are The Curious Explorer.

You want to know what the new tools can do. Good news: an AI helper is easier to try than the headlines suggest.

You are The Storykeeper.

Your family stories deserve a safe home. The right project turns them into something to hold and hand down.

You are The Confident One.

No trouble on the list fits you today. Perhaps the basics are well in hand. Or perhaps you are only starting out and do not own these devices yet: a first lesson can start from zero.

You are The Organizer.

You keep a business or a group running. Here is where AI could take some weight off.

You are The Wait-and-See Type.

Nothing on the list is pressing, and that is a fine answer. If questions about AI come up later, ask Rikki.
