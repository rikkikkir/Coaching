Approach

What Rikki believes about teaching

Nobody is “bad at technology”

Phones and computers are confusing because the makers change them every year. The confusion comes from the design, not from you.

Written steps beat a good memory

A lesson without notes fades. Every lesson ends with written steps to keep.

The learner drives

You hold the device. A skill learned with your own hands stays longer than one you only watched.

AI is a tool, not an oracle

AI can draft, explain and organize. AI can also be wrong with total confidence. Both facts belong in the first lesson.

A new practice. Rikki has taught software to coworkers for years and has helped family and friends with phones, WiFi and AI. Lessons for the public are new. Early learners get the full attention of a teacher building a practice with care.
